# Resource use

What betterlink costs, how to measure it, and what it must not exceed.

```sh
make .build/measure-resources
.build/measure-resources 60 <betterlink pid> <betterlink-tabhost pid>
```

Measure with an isolated build (CONTRIBUTING.md: `BETTERLINK_HOME`, plus `BETTERLINK_TABS_SOCKET_DIR`) against a throwaway browser, with the screen unlocked: a locked screen draws nothing, so it hides what drawing costs.

| | Budget |
|---|---|
| Idle, no browser in front | app under 0.1% of a core, under 1 wakeup a second |
| Quiet browser in front | the same |
| Several quiet browser windows, each with its sidebar | the same |
| Several windows with sidebars, another app in front, tabs changing: paced every 2 s | four windows: app under 0.5% and 1 wakeup a second with one background tab changing its title twice a second; under 2% and 2 wakeups a second with 56 tabs changing twice a second. 30 s runs with Finder in front, Chrome for Testing, on the Mac mini: one tab 0.66% before this change (at c2978e5), 0.31% after (at 536c6f2); 56 tabs 3.04% and 1.0 wakeups a second before, 1.41% and 1.2 after (the tab host helper 0.19 to 0.30%) |
| 56 tabs changing their titles twice a second, sidebar hidden | app under 2% |
| Tab host | flat memory, under 1% |

`make check-idle` fails when the built app (its Sidebar page open, so the tab sync runs) or the tab host uses more than 1% of a core or wakes more than 5 times a second with nothing happening. Sync is on in it, with a folder of its own, so its watchers and its rescan timer are covered. With `CHECK_IDLE_SIDEBAR` set to a Chromium browser's app, the script also starts that browser with a profile of its own and turns the sidebar on for it, so the sidebar's observers and its panel are covered too; the sidebar attaches to every window of that browser (resizing them and switching their tabs to vertical), so the script refuses to start while that browser is already running, and refuses an app that is not a Chromium browser. The check fails, rather than measuring less, when Sync never wrote its file or the sidebar never attached. CI runs it on every pull request with the sidebar beside Chrome; the limits are loose because CI machines are noisy. It looks three times, 15 s each, and passes on the first look within the limits: a slow machine may still be settling after the launch in the first. A look over the limits prints a `sample` of the app, so the log shows what it was doing.

How it stays there:

- **Tab lists are paced** (`PublishPacer`, unit tested). Opens, closes, moves and switches go on at once. What a tab shows (title, icon) goes at once when quiet and at most 10 times a second under churn, but only while a sidebar is on screen and an attached browser is the front app (`PublishPacer.interval`, unit tested). Otherwise every 2 seconds: with no sidebar on screen, and with sidebars on screen beside their windows while another app is in front, which is most of the day. When a browser comes to the front, what was held back goes on at once. Between those times the app does next to nothing for a title: the tab sync (`SyncHub`, `holdingContent`) reads and hands on the first title or icon change, keeps the title changes after it unread, and reads only each tab's newest when the lists are published (`TabChurnTests`: 1121 titles across 56 tabs are handed on once and decoded at most once per tab; the lists come out as reading every line would). Anything else that comes reads what was kept first, in order. The socket's reader runs the sync's turn itself rather than waking another thread for each message. A tab's new address is handed on at once, for the saved links' held tabs. The extension sends the whole tab with every update, so a line that names only the title can carry a new address too: kept unread, that one is handed on when it is read, at the next publish or when another kind of line comes, up to 2 s after it came (`TabChurnTests`).
- **Tab rows are drawn again only when something they show or use changed** (`SkipUnchanged`, `RowKey` in SidebarView): another tab's title isn't such a thing. New titles alone don't make a new look for the sidebar: each goes to its own row's title (`TabTitle`), and nothing else in the sidebar is worked out again (`SidebarLayoutTests`, which also checks it draws what a fresh sidebar draws).
- **The host drains its pool per read** (`e2e/host-memory.test.mjs` fails if its memory grows).
- **The extension says nothing** when an update changes nothing the app sees.
- **A sidebar per window adds nothing while nothing changes.** No timer runs for a sidebar at rest. Each is handed what it shows only when its own window's tabs, Browser Profile or held tabs differ (`SidebarLook`, unit tested), so a tab changing in one window redraws no other window's sidebar, and one that isn't on screen is handed nothing. Their places among the windows are checked on app and window events, with one reading of the window list for all of them, and once more 0.3 s later. Which browser window each one is, is worked out on the same kind of event (the tab lists as they are published, a window's title or resting place, the focus; a window's title is read when it changes only while its browser is in front, and behind another app when the next pairing is about to use it; which app is in front is asked when an app comes forward, not on each title; `WindowTitleReads` and `WindowTrackerFrontTests`, unit tested), once more 0.3 s after a word on the focus that could change it, and once 3 s after a pairing the focus made starts to look wrong or a focused window's title starts saying it is another window; a comparison of titles and frames, with nothing drawn unless the answer differs. For it the window server's complete list (every window of every app on every Space) is read when a window is made, closed, comes to rest or takes the focus, when an app comes forward and when the extension reports one window more or fewer: one read for all the sidebars, about 2.5 ms for 350 windows for the call alone, timed on a Mac mini outside the app, and 2.5 to 15.4 ms in the app (median 8.4 ms over 36 reads with the Mac idle and 6.4 ms over 24 with eight busy loops running; in a later run 2.6 to 15.4 ms, median 6.3 ms over 62 reads, idle and busy together; `BETTERLINK_TRACE=1` logs each read's time in the app). Never when tab lists or titles arrive, however often, and never on a timer. So a window renamed while its browser is behind another app, with no tab event to go with it, is paired again only at the next tab list or window event, not when it is renamed.
- **What kind a window is is read on events only** (`WindowKinds`, unit tested): a window is walked for its tabs when it first appears (its browser's own chrome, a few dozen elements, pages skipped; the walk stops at the first tab), and one that shows none at most five more times over three seconds. A window seen with tabs is not read again, and one judged not to be a browser window only when it is resized or comes back from the Dock. With `BETTERLINK_TRACE=1` each walk logs its round trips to the browser and its milliseconds. The browser's own word for a window costs nothing more to get: it is read off the pairing that is worked out anyway, and handed on only when it differs. A window waiting for it (only in a browser whose windows show no tabs, with the Tabs extension connected there) holds one one-shot timer of 0.6 s. Not measured in Dia: it was not run.
- **Mouse drags are watched** only while a browser is in front, or until the button comes up after a window moved with it down.
- **A Browser Profile not found is looked for less often after a minute** (`ProfileLocator.lookAgain`, unit tested): every 5 s for its first minute, then every 30 s, as a found one is read again. So a profile not found within a minute now shows up to 30 s late (it was 5 s).
- **The sync rescan** skips this Mac's own file and may run up to 10 s late.
